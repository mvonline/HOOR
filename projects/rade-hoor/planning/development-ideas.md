# Development ideas

## Wind-erased tracks and skilled tracking

Recorded: 2026-11-04. User accepted this idea for future development.
Status: deferred concept; not implemented. Chapter placement and production
scope remain to be decided when this work is scheduled.

Moving sand gradually obscures footprints and makes navigation harder. Some
characters have exceptional, learned tracking skills: reading print edges,
drift direction, depth, stride and marks on firmer surfaces. Daniyal can learn
these observations through play. Investigation should reward close observation, linking interrupted tracks, and testing a route before committing to it.

Proposed mechanics:

- Fresh tracks are readable; wind erases exposed sections faster than sheltered
  tracks behind rocks or on firm ground.
- Players connect interrupted prints with displaced stones, cloth caught on
  obstacles, hand marks and other physical clues.
- A held focus action helps inspect existing clues using framing and restrained
  lighting. Findings describe evidence, such as two travellers or a dragging
  right foot, rather than revealing an entire route automatically.
- Skilled companions explain clues that less experienced characters miss;
  Daniyal gradually learns to recognize them. Avoid assigning this ability to
  an entire ethnic or regional group.
- Losing a trail permits recovery from the last reliable clue. Wind must never
  permanently erase the only required route or make a save unwinnable.

Possible scene: two sets of tracks lead from the camp vehicle toward the tunnel.
A gust covers the exposed middle section. Sheltered fragments reveal that one
traveller limps and branches away from the main trail. This can communicate
story information through investigation rather than a dialogue explanation.

Before implementation, define the scene's narrative consequences, clue states,
wind timing, save persistence, accessibility and fallback navigation. Validate
readability and fair recovery with playtests.
